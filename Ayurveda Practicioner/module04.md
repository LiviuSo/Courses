The Influence of the Energies

In Ayurveda, it is believed that the energies animate us and the entire universe around us. Indeed,
we are not just beings made of flesh. Things that we do not understand well are part of us: for
example, we have a consciousness, urges, desires, a personality, etc. But how does this work? How
can energies influence our physical body, as well as our moods, our thoughts, our dreams, and our
character?

Actually, our body is a vast network of different energies that intersect, meet, reinforce or weaken
each other. They are at the origin of our state of mind, our qualms, and our health. Also, as we have
already mentioned, the boundaries of our body do not stop at the physical body. Our body
possesses an Aura, a subtle halo of light that serves as a barrier to external negativity, and inside of
which our energies flow. Today, we are going to take a closer look into the different layers of this
Aura.

**Perceptions of these Energies in the World**

Numerous civilizations have studied these energies. The Chakras, for example, are not just the
beliefs of ancestral Indians or a Western yoga trend of the past 30 years. In fact, the Pythagoreans,
Mayans, Egyptians, and Celts have all before described wheels of energy and a subtle form of
anatomy. In more recent times, Saint Teresa of Avila, a Carmelite nun from the 16th century, spoke
clearly about the “mansions of the body” in describing, in fact, the Chakras.
Many years later, writers and founders of new schools of thought, such as Helena Blavatsky, Alice
Bailey, Arthur Avalon, and Rudolf Steiner, for example, have also studied our energetic bodies, and
the link between the individual and the Universe. In short, we are not talking about new ideas, but
of ideas that have been mentioned across the ages and in all regions of the world.

**Perceive and Experiment Our Energies**

But to be persuaded that such a subtle thing exists in a world where we have been taught to believe
only in the material can prove to be difficult. As such, people who claim to be able to perceive
subtle bodies and Auras, are often considered strange or crazy. Indeed, one is entitled to wonder
why some people would have this ability and not others?
Well, it seems that it is all a question of training and acceptance. Therefore, if we want to progress,
we will have to remain open-minded and perseverant. Perseverant because years spent silencing
our instincts and putting up walls between our inner “self” and our social “self," will not vanish in
seconds. We will have to reawaken that part of ourselves and undo some of our reflexes and
beliefs!

**Our Subtle Anatomy: The Five Koshas**

The earliest evidence of knowledge of subtle bodies comes from Vedic India and therefore from
Ayurveda. Since that time five koshas have been described, that is five body sheaths: the physical
body and four subtle bodies.

1. The physical body (Annamaya kosha) is the physical sheath made of organic matter. It is the
   body that depends on the consumption of food, water, and oxygen.
2. The vital body (or etheric body, Pranayama kosha) is the sheath made up of the vital energy of
   Prana. It is in this body that we will detect blockages, leaks, or other energetic anomalies, and
   thus understand the state of health of the body.
3. The emotional body (Manomaya kosha) is the sheath composed of sensations and emotions. It
   is the most unstable body. A disturbance of this body will have repercussions in the two lower koshas (physical and vital bodies) and block the two higher koshas. It is in this body that our
   representation of the external world is found.
4. The mental body, the body of intelligence (Vijnanamata kosha), corresponds to the world of
   reflection. It is with this body that we reason, think, judge, analyze emotions, and make
   decisions.
5. The spiritual body (Anandamaya kosha) reveals the general orientation of a person’s life, with
   their basic psychological wounds, those which we bear from birth and which we are brought to
   transcend and heal. It is a body of light which envelops and unifies all of the others.

**The Energy Channels: The Nadis**

The Nadis, which can be translated as energy channels, are the pathways by which the vital energy Prana circulates. Like the energies that flow in us, these channels vibrate at a frequency outside of the field of what is visible to the human eye. But let us do a brief review of Prana.

Prana is our main life force; it enters into us at the moment of conception and leaves us at the time of our death. It is the Ayurvedic equivalent of Qi in Chinese medicine. Its role is to ensure the coherence of the body, mind, and soul. To illustrate this, one could say that if a person’s body, mind, and spirit are pearls on the same necklace, then Prana is the thread that supports and binds these beads together.

The seat of this vital force within our body is in our head. It is responsible for our thoughts, our memory, our emotions, and our instincts. Prana is also responsible for our breathing, and therefore of the proper oxygenation of our tissues. In fact, it controls the good physiological functioning of the heart, and thus it enters the blood to allow its oxygenation. Prana also governs all of the neural transmissions of our body (our motor and sensory nerves).

Finally, remember that Prana is provided by our diet, as well as by the air that we absorb and that we can intensify it by breathing exercises and appropriate nutrition.

So the Nadis form an extensive network of channels carrying Prana. It is very vast, for in Ayurveda, their number is symbolically set at 72,000. The energy flows through each of these vessels, and at certain intersection points will interact with other Nadis thereby intensifying its flow. This knowledge is not specific to Ayurveda. In fact, the Nadis are also used in Chinese medicine, in the form of acupuncture meridians. The large crossroads of Nadis form the Chakras, as we will see in the following chapter.

**The 3 Parts of the Brain**

As we will see later, the main Nadis are connected to our brain. They have different characteristics
depending on which part of the brain they are linked to. To understand, it is essential to know the
Ayurvedic representation of our brain!

Left hemisphere: It is the representative of solar and masculine energy. It is characterized by
intellectual virtues and discernment. In Indian mythology, this area is governed by the creative
energy of the god Brahma.

Right hemisphere: It is the representative of lunar and feminine energy. It governs our feelings
and emotions. This zone is governed by the protective energy of Vishnu.

Finally, our animal brain: It is the representative of the energy of the physical body. It governs
our instincts and is directed by the energy of destruction, Mahesha.

The 3 Essential Nadis

We will now return to the Nadis’ path. For now, we are only going to talk about the three main
Nadis. They are directly linked to each of the main seven Chakras and are essential to our
understanding of the energetic functioning of the individual. Here are the Nadis:

* Ida
* Pingala
* Sushumna

![nadis](../Ayurveda%20Practicioner/images/module04-nadis.png)

Ida and Pingala crisscross over each Chakra, around Sushumna. They follow the path of the spine, of the coccyx up to the top of the skull and the other way around.

**Nadi Ida**

Ida starts from the far right end of our coccyx, crosses the seven Chakras, irrigates the right cerebral hemisphere, before descending to finish its path in our left nostril. It is therefore closely related to the proper functioning of our right brain, the seat of our feelings and emotions. It carries the ascending Prana of the moon, a feminine energy. As a feminine energy, Ida is the channel responsible for our psychic perceptions, our mental strength, but also our creative side. This channel is the representative of our subconscious. The functioning of this channel is thus associated with the connection we have with our mother, and all that we have inherited from her. This ascending, feminine, lunar energy is related to the fundamental elements of Earth and Water. It draws from them a characteristic of freshness. It is always represented by the color blue.

If the energy circulates poorly: in the case of blockage or imbalance, our way of behaving and perceiving the world will be affected. As such, we will become passive, lazy, sad, and withdrawn into ourselves. We will have a tendency to be rigid and to think that everything was “better before.”

We will become unable to detach ourselves from family, social, and personal conditioning, or adapt to change. Indeed, our mind will constantly be in the grip of a huge doubt, of a lack of confidence in us and in life, which will ultimately prevent us from moving forward. This imbalance, if it is significant and we do not work to repair it, can worsen into depression, epilepsy, or senility.

Nadi Pingala

This channel is the opposite of Ida. It starts from the right nostril to irrigate our left cerebral hemisphere, before descending to the left side of the coccyx, also crossing each of the seven Chakras. Therefore, it is related to our left brain and gives us the virtues of reasoning, analysis and logic. It carries the descending and solar Prana, the masculine energy. Thus, it governs our physical strength, our muscular activity, and directs our consciousness towards our external environment.

Unlike Ida which focuses on our feelings and inner emotions, it represents the male principle. It is related to our relationship with our father, in terms of heredity or influence. The elements connected to this dynamic, downward, hot, solar energy are Fire and Air. This Nadi is always represented by the color red. Its heat is related to the digestive energy, the fire Agni, which we talked about in the previous module.

If this Nadi is blocked, we will find signs of a surplus of Fire, with the development of inflammatory symptoms, for example. In our behavior, we will become tyrannical, selfish, angry, and greedy.

Nadi Sushumna

This energy channel is the central channel, equidistant from Ida and Pingala. It perfectly follows the path of our spine. Because of its position, Sushumna cannot be aligned and balanced unless the other two Nadis are as well. It starts at the end of the coccyx and ends in the cervical cavity. Its energy is neutral: it is the axis of Life and Sensibility. As such, it represents peace and the end of duality. It represents the accomplished individual, the perfect channel for the energies, having grown progressively, and having found total harmony between its masculine and feminine references and energies, between father and mother.

In Ayurveda, Sushumna is considered to be the most important channel. Indeed, it is the channel of the Kundalini, the pure divine energy, represented in the form of a coiled snake. The Kundalini is the energy that connects us to the divine, to perfection, and to the whole universe. It is present in every individual. It can be awakened by seeking to evolve our consciousness with the help of breathing exercises, and various energy techniques.

Ayurvedic Wisdom

During the day, we breathe in an alternating fashion, with one nostril dominating the other. The body balances the energies by alternating the dominant nostril every 90 to 150 minutes. The left nostril is linked to calm and relaxation. It is associated with Yin, with the feminine side, with cold, with change, and with the energy from the moon. The right nostril is more active and alert. It is associated with Yang, with the masculine, with heat, and with solar energy.
Conscious breathing implies that we must lend attention to the dominant nostril. This is especially true if you are too active, too stressed, and you want to calm down. To reverse one energy, block the nostril associated with that energy, and breathe through the other nostril for at least 5 minutes.

The Energy Centers: The Chakras

You may be somewhat familiar with the word "Chakra." If you practice yoga, you probably have an idea of what they correspond to. Chakra is a Sanskrit word that translates into wheel or disk. The 7 most important Chakras are found in the Sushumna. They are often represented by double cones.

The junction of these two cones is found within Sushumna, and the openings flare out forward and backward in the body.

![chakras](../Ayurveda%20Practicioner/images/module04-chakras.png)

Thus, they are part of the Nadis energy system that we saw earlier. We could make an analogy between the Nadis, which would be the highway network of our energies, and the Chakras, which would be the interchanges or traffic circles. As on the road, our energies can also meet traffic jams, especially at the intersections, that is the Chakras, and therefore find themselves blocked.

Understanding the Chakra system is essential to finding balance. The techniques of breathing, meditation, and yoga, which we will soon learn, make us produce a large amount of energy. Yet, for this to be correctly used we will have to learn how to steer it, and for that, we must understand the Chakras. In fact, we count many more Chakras than the 7 that we present to you here. But these are the main and most significant energy centers, and if we know how to understand and regulate them, then the rest will follow.

![chakras2](../Ayurveda%20Practicioner/images/module04-chakras2.png)

Thus, the Chakras are all located on the Nadi Sushumna, along the spine. Each Chakra is associated
with a particular color and shape. But that’s not all. Each Chakra is also associated with different
glands or organs in our body, as well as the 7 senses and "perceptions," as well as the 7 Dhatus (the
tissues that make up our body).

Each Chakra is also related to a degree of awakening of consciousness. If we are interested in the
senses, for example, the lower the degree of awakening of our consciousness, the more we will be
reassured and trust our concrete senses, like smell, touch, etc. Conversely, if our consciousness is
raised to a high level, it will make us aware of our intuitions, and to attain a state of bliss and
detachment. Here are the seven main Chakras:

Muladhara or Root Chakra

This Chakra is the lowest in our body, it is found in the region of the perineum and the anus. It is
related to the functioning of the Bartholin’s glands (which ensure the secretion of vaginal fluid in
women), the Skene’s glands (at the G-spot in women), the prostate (ensuring the production of
seminal fluid in men), and the testicles. Thus, it has a strong connection to the life force, and is
related to the basic needs of the human body, to movement, and to the relationship to nature.
The Dhatu (one of the constituent tissues of the organism) which is attached to it, is the blood
plasma. As a reminder, plasma is the first of the Dhatus, because it allows the nutrients from the
digestion of food to circulate throughout the body. It is therefore essential to the operation and
development of the following Dhatus.

Its color is red. Its energy comes from the Earth, and if it is cut off from it, it will seek this energy in
others, by demanding a lot of attention, for example. Thus, when imbalanced, this Chakra can
trigger fear, ignorance, material attachment, brutality, excessive hunger and need for sleep.

On the other hand, when balanced, it is the source of confidence, reliability, courage, and proper
management of resources. As we have seen, in our body, the Earth element is related to the sense
of smell. So this is the Chakra of smell. A little trick to recognize the people in whom this chakra is
the strongest, the most representative of their level of enlightenment: they often use terms
reflecting security, such as "credible," "solid," "can count on you or on me, "etc.

Svadhisthana, or the Seat of the Self

This energy center is located in the lower belly area. It is linked to the reproductive organs, the
women’s ovaries, the urinary organs, and the kidneys. Its Dhatu is the blood. This Dhatu allows
oxygen to move to the different tissues and organs and thus enables life. Its color is orange. Its
element is Water, and its sense is taste.
Furthermore, this element is very connected to our emotions, so when this Chakra is imbalanced, it
can lead to emotional fits, restlessness, a great emotionalism, as well as dysfunction connected to
inhibition, helplessness, or to the need for control.
On the other hand, when balanced, it contributes to happiness, good relationships with close ones,
ease in adapting to change, and a flexible mind. It is the source of aptness in our actions. This
Chakra is the Chakra of desires. Thus, the expressions used by the people in this stage of awakening
often evoke a positive appreciation: nice, super, neat, cool, funny, pretty, etc.

Manipura, the City of Jewels

This Chakra is found at the level of our solar plexus. It is related to the functioning of our digestive
system, our liver, our gallbladder, as well as the adrenal glands and the pancreas. The solar plexus is
also the place of our positive and negative emotions. One of its functions is the feeling and the
communication of emotions.

So, when we refuse the expression of emotion, be it love, tenderness, or anger, because of our
ideas or our fear of being judged, we block the functioning of this Chakra. We stiffen it. We then
build a shell that cuts us off from others, but also from ourselves. Conversely, if this Chakra is too
weak, it can be flooded by emotion and be incapable of managing it. We must find a happy
medium.

The Dhatu linked to this Chakra corresponds to the muscles. They cover the vital and non-vital
organs, enable their movement, their proper functioning, as well as physical strength and vigor of
the body. Its color is yellow. Its element is Fire, its sense is sight. With an imbalance of this Chakra
we find characteristics that correspond to the Pitta dosha. Thus, its imbalance causes anger,
jealousy, and a tendency to want to control everything and to criticize everything.

When balanced, it gives us a sense of responsibility, makes us helpful, and enables us to properly
manage events with great accuracy. When it is very well balanced, the emotion is simply felt and is
neither denied nor restrained. It is felt before fading, naturally.

This is the Chakra of Transformation. The terms used by people at this stage of awakening are
words such as: "in any case," "concretely," "promise," "one hundred percent," etc. They are terms
that show that they grab the bull by the horns, that they are men and women of action.

Anahata, or “Which Never Stops”
This Chakra is within our cardiac plexus. It is related to our heart, our cardiovascular system, our
lungs, as well as our thymus, which plays a significant role in our immunity. The Dhatu of this
Chakra is the body fat, which enables the hydration of tissues and the lubrication of joints.
Its color is green. The element of this Chakra is Air: there is a reason that it is linked to the lungs and
the blood network that carries the oxygen. The sense that is attached to this element, and thus this
Chakra, is touch. In the case of imbalance, this Chakra can bring feelings of pity, fanaticism, sadness,
and guilt.

When balanced, it brings unconditional love, devotion, sincerity, and compassion. Incidentally,
certain popular expressions reflect well this balance/imbalance: “to have a heavy heart," "to have a
heart of gold," "to open one’s heart," "to be good-hearted", etc.
It is often here that we feel the emotions linked to attachment, love, compassion, etc. It is the
chakra of gratitude. Unfortunately, it is also this chakra that bears the scars of all our emotional
wounds. Because of them, it will stiffen, close, and leave us with a “heavy heart." This is the Chakra
of Love, and the words that we find on this level of consciousness are words like "warm,"
"pleasant," "beautiful," etc.

Vishuddha, or “That Which is Pure”

We find this Chakra in the level of our throat. It is linked to the arms, the neck, the phonatory
apparatus, and the thyroid glands. So thyroid imbalances are often caused by the dysfunction of
this Chakra. The Dhatu of this Chakra corresponds to the bones, which bring structure and support
to all the other Dhatus of the body.
Its color is light blue. Its element is Ether, which connects to the sense of hearing and to sounds.
Since this Chakra is linked to the phonatory apparatus, in the case of imbalance, it brings a
tendency toward gossip, stuttering, and logorrhea. In fact, we can realize the presence of this
Chakra when our throat tightens, dries out or when the words will not come out.
When balanced, it confers a clear speech, charisma, elegance, good creative ability, etc. This is the
Chakra of the right communication, which has a purpose. The terms used at this level of awakening
are "original", "divine", "perfect", “unprecedented”, etc.

Ajna, the Command Center

It is located between the eyebrows. It is linked to the forehead, the nervous system, the epiphysis,
and the pineal gland. The latter plays a role in our vision. So the fact that it is related to this Chakra,
also called the third eye, is not a coincidence. Its Dhatu is made up of nerves and marrow. It carries
nerve impulses from the brain to the body, and vice versa.
Its color is dark blue. Its "element" is no longer a fundamental element since it consists of the mind.
The sense that we attach to it is Intuition. This Chakra is that of discernment, and in case of
imbalance, it leads to dogmatism, guilt, and inability to get our priorities straight. We lack foresight,
are rather egocentric, seek to satisfy our own interests, stay fixated on our habits, on our safety.
We do not accept the hazards of life, nor the pains of existence.

When balanced, it gives us great abilities of concentration, observation, intuition, a lively intellect,
etc. As such, when it functions properly, it gives us a clear and fair view of situations. It brings an
elevated vision of things and beings, and an understanding of the meaning of life. In this, it is a real
springboard to the highest states of consciousness.

The terms used by people at this state of awakening reflect a questioning of the truth and of their
own knowledge. The words used at this level of awakening are: "probably," "possible," "as far as I
know," "to my knowledge," etc.

Sahasrara, the Lotus of a Thousand Petals

This Chakra is located at the top of our head; it opens like a funnel and connects us to the celestial
energies. It is connected to the brain, the pituitary gland, and the hypothalamus.
Its color is purple. Its element is pure cosmic energy. The sense attributed to it is the capacity to be
present at the moment, to "just be." Thus, in imbalance, it brings all the opposite: dissatisfaction, a
negation of the deep meaning of existence and of all that happens outside the visible.
When balanced, it is simply peace, serenity, joy, and the ability to speak little, but with a strong
impact. It is the Chakra of contentment, and the most-used words by people having attained this
level of awakening of consciousness reflect it very well: "enough," "everything is clear," etc.
