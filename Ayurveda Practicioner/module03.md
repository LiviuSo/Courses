The Principles Regulating the Activity of our Body

Before discussing the concepts of disease, it’s important that we talk about the biological
functioning of the body. Thus, we will be able to understand how the structures of the body are
linked together, and how they are affected by any imbalances that may occur.
In this section, we will discuss the inner fire, or Agni, which is the main actor in the metabolism. We
will also talk about body waste, known as the three Malas. Then, we will look at the seven tissues
that form the human body, called the seven dhatus, and how they are related to each other. Finally,
we will return to the doshas, the elements which make up our personalities that we have already
discussed. We will get deeper into their roles in the body’s biological functioning.

The Inner Fire: Agni

Agni could be defined as the biological fire. It controls the metabolism, because the stronger the
fire, the more powerful the metabolism. As you may recall, the dosha that governs the metabolism
is Pitta. This dosha is effectively the only one that consists of the fundamental element of fire. It is
therefore unsurprisingly carrying the energy of Fire called Agni.
However, Agni does not depend solely on this dosha. Indeed, just like in nature, there is no Fire
without Air. Fire needs air to be lit. Thus, Agni needs Vata, the only dosha formed of the
fundamental element of Air. However Vata represents all that moves in our body due to Air, so Agni
also plays a role in the movements of our body.

Role of Agni in the Body

When it comes to the stomach, Agni, the bearer of heat and acidity, stimulates the digestion and
breaks down food into nutrients that are absorbable by the body. As such, Agni is responsible for
the nutrition of the different tissues of the body, as well as the immune response mechanism. It is
this fire that will, while in the stomach, destroy micro-organisms, foreign bacteria, and toxins. It
also protects the gut flora.

Because of these roles, Agni is the guarantor of our longevity. The better our metabolism and our
immune function, the longer we live, but also, the better our quality of life. A well-protected and
well-nourished body and brain give us better health, better perception, better comprehension, etc.

Malfunctions of Agni

Agni can be affected by an imbalance of the doshas (the facets of our personality). By affecting
Agni, this imbalance will weaken our metabolism and our immune system. As a result, a proper
breakdown of foods into nutrients cannot take place, and certain undigested components of food
will accumulate in the large intestine, forming a mass of a very sticky, odorous substance, called
Amas. Etymologically, this term comes from A: close, and Mas: poison. It is, therefore, a poison-like
substance, and you will soon understand why.

In fact, this substance clogs the intestines, as well as the capillaries and blood vessels, hindering
some of the body’s processes and causing chemical changes. It will cause the creation of toxins that
circulate the body and accumulate in the weakest parts. In Ayurveda, it is thought that the root of
any disease is Amas. This substance can develop because of many factors, such as the consumption
of food incompatible with our doshic constitution. Agni will be affected by the toxins caused by
Amas, or by the ingestion of foods that are difficult to digest.

As we have already seen, according to Ayurveda, the body and mind are connected. This is also true
for the creation of Amas. It is believed that these toxins can also be created by emotional factors.
More precisely, it is thought that all repressed emotions cause harm to the body, because this
repression prevents mind/body movement, and therefore result in a Vata imbalance, which as we
have seen is related to Agni. This imbalance affects our immune system and our metabolism, which
will be weakened, leading to illness.

However, in time of balance, when everything is going well, Amas does not have time to form
because waste is eliminated in natural ways. Let’s look at how the three Malas, or three body

The Three Malas

Our body excretes three products or three different types of waste. Despise their unflattering translation of "waste," they play an essential role in the body. They are necessary for its functioning and are good indicators of our state of health. These three products include solid material (stool), and two liquid materials (urine and sweat).

Stool – Purisha

Urine and fecal matter are issued by the large intestine where the assimilation, absorption, and selection of different nutrients takes place. The solid waste thus produced is then transported for evacuation to the rectum in the form of stool. Feces is essential for the proper functioning of the intestines because it allows them to keep their tone and shape. In a way, it helps avoid their collapse. That’s why it’s easier to live with constipation than diarrhea. Diarrhea can even be fatal if it goes on for too long, while constipation can be tolerated for a longer time, sometimes years.

Urine – Mutra

The remaining liquid part is taken to the kidneys for filtration, and the residual waste is then transported to the bladder in the form of urine. This Mala is essential for maintaining blood volume and thus good blood pressure. It is, therefore, necessary to ensure its proper production. To do this, we must maintain good hydration and a healthy diet, as well as an excellent mental and physical condition.

Sweat – Sweda

The last Mala, sweat, allows the good health of our skin and helps to regulate our body
temperature. It’s essential that this Mala is at its right balance. Indeed, excessive sweat will lower
body temperature and can lead to skin infections and dehydration. Conversely, if this Mala is
produced in too small quantity, the skin will be weakened, rough, and lacking in tone and elasticity.

The 7 Tissues of the Body: The Seven dhatus

We will now see what our body is formed of, and what it looks like as a coherent whole. This is
done thanks to the seven different tissues, the seven dhatus.

Indeed, these tissues (or dhatus) are responsible for the entire structure of the body. They regulate
the functioning of the organs, the nervous system, the respiratory system, and other vital elements
of the body. Before going into more detail of their functioning, let’s look at what these tissues are.

* Plasma (Rasa) is the first of the dhatus because it allows nutrients from the digestion of food to
  circulate throughout the body. It’s therefore indispensable to the functioning and development
  of all of the following dhatus. The waste of this tissue forms the mucus.
* Blood (Rakta) transports oxygen to the different tissues and organs, so it keeps us alive. The
  substance of its waste is bile.
* Muscles (Mamsa) cover all organs, whether vital or not, allow their movement and therefore
  their action and proper functioning. They also make up the physical force and strength of the
  body. Their waste is all of the substances from the body’s orifices (earwax, nasal secretions,
  smegma, etc.).
* Bodily fat (Meda) allows for hydration of the tissues and lubrication of the joints. Its waste
  forms sweat.
* Bones (Asthi) provide structure and support to the entire body. Their waste is hair, nails, and
  beards.
* Nerves and marrow (Majja) carry both motor and sensory nerve impulses. Their waste is tears.
* Reproductive tissues (Shukra and Artav) are the necessary materials or tissues needed for
  reproduction. This dhatu is the only one without any associated waste.

Relationship Between the 7 dhatus

The digested food will, under the effect of Agni, become nutrients of the dhatus. Then, these
nutrients will pass from one dhatu to the next, according to a specific order. They will go from
plasma to blood, then to muscles and so on. This phenomenon is important because the nutrients
that arrive to the body fat, for example, are useless unless they have passed through the three
previous tissues.

Therefore, each dhatu absorbs and assimilates the nutrients that it needs for its own functioning
and absorbs and then rejects those that are not useful to it. In fact, the nutrients must undergo the
following three actions to be used by "their" dhatu:

* Irrigation: that is to say the diffusion of nutrients by the plasma to all the dhatus.
* Selection: each dhatu extracts only the nutrients it needs to function and/or grow.
* Transformation: Whenever nutritional substances pass through a dhatu, they allow the
  production of food for the next dhatu.

The dhatus are connected to the doshas, which are in turn determined by our personality type. As
such, they are affected in the case of imbalance of the doshas and become defective. This is how
diseases occur in the body.

Good health results from a good state of the dhatus, itself guaranteed by a balance between the
doshas. This balance being the result of our lifestyle and dietary habits. We will discuss all of this in
detail in the following modules. But we see once again that in Ayurveda, the connection between
body and mind is obvious and underlies all the phenomena of disease or good health that may
occur in our lives.

The 3 Vital Forces of the Human Body

Before discussing the three vital forces, we need to briefly talk about the Aura. Aura is the name given to a subtle halo of light that surrounds all living beings. This phenomenon is mentioned in many religions, philosophies, across the ages, and throughout the world.

In Ayurveda, it is believed that the Aura is connected to the channels (Nadis) of the pranic system and to the Chakras that we will soon study. For now, we need to understand that this Aura has different shapes, intensities, and colors, and gives us information about the personality, health, and mood of the being to whom it belongs.

But that’s not all. As we will see later, the Aura has a vital role to play in our health as it protects us
from the negativity of the world around us. Only a few people are able to perceive it, following
years of training, practice, and increased sensitivity.

The Role of Ojas

Ojas governs the ability of our organism to defend itself from external aggression, whether physical
or psychic. Ojas is, therefore, the basis of our immune system. It allows our good health and gives
us strength and vigor. Thus, if this force declines in us, our immune system will be weakened, and
we will get sick more easily.
On the psychic plane, this vital force is the foundation of our Aura. One of the roles of the Aura is
precisely to be our first line of defense against the outside. It functions as a barrier that protects us
from the negativity projected on us consciously or unconsciously by the outside world.
This is why when Ojas is powerful in us. It reinforces our "Ahamkara," that is, our integrity and
identity. The stronger this identity, the less it will let Amas, the toxic energy, form.

Ojas Balance and Imbalance

This force is closely related to the Kapha dosha (one of the three main components of our
personality). Because of this link, if Kapha is in an imbalance, the Ojas life force will be displaced,
and therefore will no longer be able to perform its usual role.
Kapha dosha disorders, such as diabetes, increased cholesterol levels, limb numbness, or fragile
bones and joints will occur.

On the other hand, if Ojas is weakened, the problems that will appear will be of the Vata dosha
type. The individual will then be subject to sensations of fear and weakness, and their senses will be
numb. They may also suffer from loss of consciousness, which could even lead to their death.
In fact, as soon as Ojas is weakened or moved, it leaves room for Amas. As we saw earlier, Amas is a
substance similar to poison and perfect for the development of toxins, bacteria, viruses, and
cancerous cells. Because of the presence of Amas, the more Ojas is weakened, the more an internal
disharmony will form. This is true on the physical plane, but also on the psychic plane.
Therefore, a decrease in our vital force Ojas will undermine our aura. Now, if our aura weakens, we
will become more susceptible to ambient negativity, fear, doubt, anxiety, and so on. And inevitably,
by being fed negativity, we will develop it in ourselves. In fact, one could illustrate the interaction
between Ojas and Amas like the water and the air present in a glass.

An empty glass is, after all, always filled with air. But, if we pour water into the glass, we reduce the
amount of air it contains. Then, as soon as we drink it, we take away part of the water in the glass,
and it is immediately replaced by air. In the same manner, when Ojas decreases, Amas increases in
us. And if it gets stronger, then Amas is reduced.

When Ojas is powerful, the vital force Tejas (we will see this in more detail later) is able to ensure
efficient digestion and proper assimilation, both in the physical and the psychic sense. Our
emotions and food will then be managed as peacefully and efficiently as possible, ensuring us a
serene state of mind and an ideal biological functioning.

When Ojas is well balanced, it guarantees a very satisfactory physical and psychic state. Our health
is excellent, and our emotions are very positive.

**How Can We Strengthen Ojas?**

All Ayurvedic treatments are thought to increase Ojas in the body. The factors that can increase
Ojas, and which must, therefore, be present in everyday life, are:

* Level of consciousness: The principal factor that determines the production of Ojas in a person
  is his state of consciousness. The more the “inner self” is awakened, the more Ojas will be
  produced. Therefore, achieving peace and happiness is the best way to generate Ojas.
* Proper digestion and a balanced diet: Ayurvedic advice on nutrition has the goal of increasing
  Ojas because Ojas is seen as the end product of perfect digestion and a good metabolism.
* Positive emotions and feelings: Love, joy, affection, etc.… These type of feelings increase Ojas
  and thus confer a better immunity.
* Panchakarma, or Ayurvedic purification therapy: This removes impurities from the body and
  enhances the ability of cells to receive Ojas and regenerate the body.
* Rasayanas, or herbal therapy: They also help to produce more Ojas in the body.

On the other hand, the following factors reduce Ojas and should be avoided:

* Negative emotions: Negative emotions such as anger, violence, offensive or hateful speech,
  vanity, gossip, selfishness, dishonesty, greed, etc. are all factors that largely reduce the
  production of Ojas.
* Stress: Always having a sense of urgency.
* Excessive physical activity: The right amount of exercise varies from one person to another. The
  feeling of weakness that someone may feel after they exercise too much is a manifestation of
  the reduction of Ojas.
* Fasting: Moderate fasting can absolutely be done, but only in a very temporary way and in
  situations that are supervised by an Ayurveda practitioner. On the other hand, fasting for too
  long or too regularly can considerably weaken the body by depleting the body’s tissues and
  reducing Ojas, whose role is to feed them.
* Diet regimes that are too unsubstantial: Diets that do not offer a large enough quantity of food,
  calories, and nutrients will weaken the body and drastically reduce Ojas. This is also true in
  extreme situations, such as in cases of anorexia.
* Other factors: For example, the overexposure to wind or sun, staying up too late, significant loss
  of bodily fluids, excessive sexual activity, wounds or physical trauma, alcoholic beverages.

We must, therefore, find what works best for us in terms of way of life, diet, etc. We must also find
harmony and inner peace. And for this, we know a simple, free, effective technique that is available
to everyone: meditation. We will study that more in a following module.

**PRANA**

This vital force is made up of the fundamental elements of Air and Ether. It is indispensable to light the fire Agni and therefore the life force Tejas. If you are interested in Chinese medicine, Prana is the equivalent of Chi. It is our main life force, entering into us at the moment of conception, and leaving us at the moment of death. Its role is to ensure the coherence of the body, mind, and soul.

To illustrate this, one could say that if a person's body, mind, and soul are pearls on the same necklace, then Prana is the string that binds these pearls together.


Prana’s Role

Ayurveda considers Prana’s primary seat to be in the head. As such, this force is responsible for our thoughts, our memory, our emotions, as well as our instincts - which we ignore so often as adults, but which controlled us as children.

For example, a child who lacks iron or calcium, will naturally, without judgment, put dirt into his mouth, of course, not for the taste or because of curiosity (except maybe for the first bite), but just as a source of minerals within reach.

This child’s Prana tells his body to consume it in order to replenish his stock of iron and calcium. Unfortunately, as adults, we no longer know how to listen to or follow our instincts. More often than not, we ignore them, prioritizing instead social norms. Ayurveda teaches us to reconnect with our instincts.

Prana also controls our breathing, and therefore the proper oxygenation of our tissues. It controls the physiological functioning of the heart, and through it, it enters the blood and allows its oxygenation. Moreover, this force also governs all the neural transmissions of our body.

**How To Recharge Prana?**

This vital force is brought to us by the air that we breathe, as well as the food that we consume. That said, not all nutrients allow us to recharge Prana. This is the case only for those that are assimilated into the colon. According to scientific research, only one type of food is absorbed on this level: volatile fatty acids.

These fatty acids are the product of the microbial fermentation of the fiber that we ingest. That is, if the fiber intake consumed increases, our gut will start producing more of these volatile fatty acids, and therefore strengthen our Prana. Once again, excess is always dangerous: indeed, too much of these acids can cause neurological diseases.

Here again, everything is a question of balance. The amount of Prana that we can absorb will depend on the health of our lungs and our colon. The sign that Prana is balanced is vitality and zest for life.

Role of Tejas

As we learned in module 1, Ayurveda considers the human being as a whole uniting the body, the
mind, and the soul. All three function together in a coherent whole. They influence and
communicate with each other thanks to Tejas. This vital force is connected to the digestive fire
Agni. It determines the metabolism and is necessary for all cellular processes, as well as to
maintaining some homeostasis in the body.

It also allows the feeding and development of the seven tissues (dhatus) composing the human
body and ensures their proper physiological functioning. On the psychological level, it ensures the
“digestion” of emotions, ideas, and thoughts. Finally, Tejas is also responsible for how we choose
our priorities. Thus, if you are assaulted one evening when returning from the restaurant, it is
thanks to Tejas that your digestion will be put on hold so that all the energy dedicated to it can be
redirected to your muscles, and thus allow you to flee or fight.

Balance and Imbalance of Tejas

The three vital forces influence and balance each other. Indeed, Tejas protects and helps to create
Ojas, which in turn supports Prana. Thus, if one of these forces is negatively affected, the other two
will also be, by a domino effect. For example, if Tejas is too strong in the body, then Prana’s activity
will be overstimulated, and the fire of Tejas (Agni) will burn Ojas. As a result, the immune system
will be weakened, and the person will get sick.

On the other hand, a weak Tejas will cause the growth of unhealthy tissue or tumors, which will
hinder the circulation of Prana. As for Ojas, it will then be overproduced and unregulated. But as for
all, although Ojas is an excellent force when in balance when it is in surplus, the body does not
know how to handle it. It then transforms it into waste, into Amas, and it becomes toxic to the
body. To maintain a good balance, as always in Ayurveda, it will be necessary to acquire good daily
habits and a good diet in agreement with our deep doshic nature.

Tejas, for example, will be particularly affected by the use of narcotics. All harmful habits which are
not adapted to our doshic composition can thus lead to an aggravation of Tejas. Balanced Tejas will
manifest itself physically by the radiance of the skin, eyes, and hair, and great charisma. People who
are able to see the auras can see it in the form of a halo.
